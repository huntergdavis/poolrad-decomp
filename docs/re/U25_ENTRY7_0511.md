# Unit 0x25 +0x0511: provisional full-state public entry 7

`pr_u25_entry7_0511` translates the complete U25 public entry 7 (far stub
`2B:0043`) over caller-owned A20-off 1 MiB RAM and a fourteen-word CPU
frame. Its two far callees in unit `0x3B9` are fail-closed call-site
callbacks. **Game identity is not asserted, and no strict full-C,
identity, count or native credit is requested.**

## Original boundary and ownership

Ignored DOS v1.3 `GAME.OVR` is 206,105 bytes, SHA-256
`872964a32cef0fc32bbf83bab68b15e04ad36d59076f37b00a179dfee7928614`.
Unit `0x25` starts at file `0x74D3`. The executable routine is exactly file
`[0x79E4,0x7A17)`, unit `+0511..+0543`, **51 bytes / 21 instructions**,
SHA-256 `755c5d1d6e77aaa70982d05d3c9b0b9b985fe19beac8387ecc94a9d8070c2500`.
It has no jumps, and its only return is `+0541 RETF 4`. It follows
`+0482`'s `RETF 6`.

The ledger CSV's 61-byte bound wrongly includes `+0544..+054D`. Those 10
bytes are data owned by `+054E`: its `MOV DI,0544` at `+059E` and
`MOV DI,054A` at `+064D` are the only references, and `+054E`'s prologue
follows them.

The callers are the far call through `2B:0043` at U1C `+090C`, inside the
PICTURE handler ([U1C_PICTURE_0856.md](U1C_PICTURE_0856.md)), and the near
`PUSH CS; CALL` at `+0699` in `+054E`. pF (i8911) and pG (i8971) cleared
this interval relative to their own work.

| Site | Callee | Arguments | Cleanup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `+052D` | `3D0:003E` = U3B9 `+05D9` entry 6 | `[BP+8]`, then `[BP+6]` (bytes, AH carried) | `RETF 4` |
| `+053B` | `3D0:0039` = U3B9 `+0697` entry 5 | 3, 3, 1 (bytes, AH carried) | `RETF 6` |

The stub-to-entry mapping is offset `0x25 + 5·(slot−1)`. An earlier screen
of mine named entries 4 and 3 here; that was corrected in i8987. Both
`RETF` sizes were checked from the callee bytes.

## Behavior

1. Save BP.
2. Clear `DS:[84E1]`, then copy argument byte `[BP+8]` to `DS:[49FD]` and
   argument byte `[BP+6]` to `DS:[49FE]`.
3. Call entry 6 with those two bytes.
4. Call entry 5 with 3, 3, 1.
5. `POP BP` from the live stack word (there is no `MOV SP,BP`), then
   `RETF 4`.

No instruction in the body writes flags. Each hook sees the flags that
reached it, and the return carries entry 5's flags.

Arguments and saved words are re-read from RAM where the original reads
them. SS/DS aliasing therefore takes effect in source order, including
writes to `[84E1]`, `[49FD]` or `[49FE]` that land in the frame, and callee
writes to the saved BP or the return words.

## Verification

`test_overlay_u25_entry7_0511.c` checks:

- the three global writes
- both argument layouts, including AH carry
- the flags each hook sees and the return flags
- the return frame
- a live saved-BP rewrite by a callee
- an abort at each call, with no later effect
- argument rejection

It passes strict C11 `-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Werror` and ASan+UBSan.

`check_overlay_u25_entry7_0511.py` pins:

- the file and body hashes and the 21 instructions
- both call targets and the absence of jumps
- the sole `RETF 4`
- `+054E`'s ownership of the trailing data

Its source pins have run statically. Its Unicorn differential has **not**
run and waits for a PM serial slot. When run, it covers 80 cases:

- argument patterns under normal, DS-alias (frame over `84E1` or `49FD`),
  low and high stack offsets
- callee return-word rewrites
- 40 seeded cases

For each case it compares all 1 MiB of RAM, the CPU words and both call
traces, and requires every instruction start.

No reached native `+0511` frame was searched. Callee and caller native
parity remain open.
